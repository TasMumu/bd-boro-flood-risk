# 6. Discussion

## 6.1 What can and cannot be claimed

One claim is strongly supported as a description of a single event. In the 2017 flood, the loss in BBS statistics appeared in harvested area and production, and almost none in yield. The dose-response model in both records, the year-by-year comparison and synthetic control agree in direction, although the planned synthetic-control test failed and the JRC estimate depends on 2017. BBS's own damage reports, a satellite measure that uses no BBS data and published figures point to the same districts and to losses of similar scale, and BBS reported far less Boro damage in 2016 and 2022, when no comparable fall appears. This is strong evidence consistent with flood-related crop destruction, although no single comparison proves that the whole fall in area was caused by the flood.

The broader claim, that flash floods generally reduce Boro through area, is not established. Under the plan's rule the evidence is suggestive, and the estimate rests on 2016 and 2017. Two questions need to be kept apart: whether flood exposure is associated with lost harvested area, and whether the size of that loss is stable. The first has support, particularly for 2017. The second is much weaker (Table 15): the Sentinel-1 estimate falls by a third or more when small districts are left out or larger districts get more weight, and it then lies closer to JRC. This bears on how flood effects are read elsewhere: with few events and a few treated districts, an estimate from a large panel can be one event in disguise.

## 6.2 Why area and not yield

The likely reason is how the statistics are built. BBS estimates yield from crop cuts on plots in fields growing the crop, and area separately [@bbs2026]. Its report for 2016–17 describes flash flood as fully damaging crop on 296,701 ha, its national area fell by the same amount, and it attributes higher yield to management [@bbs2018, p. 68]. So in 2017 destroyed crop left the area estimate, and the yield estimate reflects the crop that remained. Since Boro is planted before flash floods arrive, a same-season fall in harvested area means crop lost and not a planting decision. The yearbooks do not state this as a general rule, and it cannot be assumed for every year: in 2016, reported damage is not fully visible in area. The small positive yield effect with JRC could mean that weaker fields are lost first, but the evidence for this is thin.

This does not contradict Li et al. (2025), who report yield losses from submergence worldwide. Li et al. and this study measure different things in different statistical systems. Whether crop that is totally lost appears in yield or area depends on how each country's data are collected. Similarly, national data may fail to show flood effects at all [@lesk2016], because a loss such as 2017 is confined to a few districts. Our results differ from the Kansas wheat findings [@obembe2021] and resemble those on harvested ratios [@cui2020], which suggests that the answer depends on the crop, hazard and data system.

## 6.3 Implications

**Disaster assessment.** This recommendation follows directly from our results. Yield alone is a poor signal of the largest flood losses in this system. Sunamganj lost {{ f(abs(E17.loc['Sunamganj', 'production_t_chg_pct']), 0) }} % of its production in 2017 with no visible change in yield. Assessments should track harvested area and production alongside yield, and research on Bangladesh's rice should not treat stable yields as evidence of low flood risk. BBS's damage tables are a useful source, but we found them for few years. A routine estimate of planted area before the flood window would show directly what share of the crop was lost.

**Satellite monitoring.** Greenness-based measures located the 2017 damage without official data, as earlier work found for the same event [@ahmed2017], and similar methods have been used elsewhere [@rahman2020a; @qamer2023]. They are useful for rapid assessment, but the 2016 mismatch shows that a fall in greenness should not be read as crop loss without information on harvest progress. NDVI alone should not be used to infer yield shocks in a district.

**Timing and adaptation.** Recommendations in this area come mainly from earlier work, and our exploratory timing result is consistent with them without establishing them. Earlier sowing and short-duration varieties are recommended so that harvest finishes before the flood window [@hossain2023], though farmers face barriers to adopting them [@baishakhy2023]. Flood-tolerant rice has improved outcomes in north-west Bangladesh [@bairagi2021], but that evidence does not transfer directly to Boro in the haor, where the threat comes at maturity. Interest in crop insurance exists [@islam2021], and our results suggest that a payout tied to district yield would not have been triggered in 2017, so any index for the haor should consider area or satellite-observed loss.

## 6.4 Limitations

The study covers 15 years and few large flash floods, and one event dominates, so the findings describe these events and do not give a general law. Floods are not randomly assigned. BBS harvested area comes from official estimates, and BBS's damage reports are not independent of them. Other evidence checks them only partly and only for large events, and printed values can contain errors, as Chandpur's 2017 area appears to. The analysis plan has no public timestamp from before the results. The Boro mask relies on a fixed NDVI rule and a static cropland map, is not validated with field data, and has noisy yearly totals. Sentinel-1 has fewer scenes before 2017, and controlling for scene counts does not remove every consequence of uneven coverage. Optical records such as JRC can miss short floods that radar observes [@uddin2019; @twele2016]. Flood fractions in the main models cover whole districts, and the required rice-only check weakened the estimates. Satellite greenness loss cannot separate destruction from early harvest. Cold-stress thresholds are provisional. The risk index omits adaptive capacity and salinity [@dasgupta2017], so it is a partial picture, and its rank ranges are wide. It should guide where to look more closely, and should not be used as an allocation rule.
